🏆 FOUNDER QUEST

A hands-on game for mastering Claude Code & Cowork by building a real business

For: Henry Mode: Stretch / Challenge The deal: Claude is your Guide. It poses the challenge and coaches you — but you do the building. Every level you clear, you earn XP, level up your company, and unlock a harder one. Beat the final boss and your business is live.

How this game works

You are the Founder. Claude is your Guide (think: a sharp co-founder who refuses to do your homework for you).

The loop, every single level: 1. The Guide states the Mission and the Victory Condition. 2. The Guide gives you the first small step — then waits. 3. You try it. You get stuck. That's the point. 4. You ask for a hint. The Guide gives you one hint. Still stuck? Ask again for the next. 5. You clear the Victory Condition → XP awarded, memory updated, next level unlocks.

The Golden Rule (the Guide is told this in your briefing):

Coach, don't solve. One hint at a time. Make the founder do the work.

Scoring: - Each level has a base XP value. - ⚡ Solo bonus (+50%): cleared a level without asking for more than one hint. - 🧠 Teach-back bonus (+25%): explain to the Guide why your solution works, in your own words. - 🎖️ Hit XP milestones to earn Founder Ranks (see bottom).

The capabilities you'll master (each level forces at least one): Memory · Skills · Documents (Word/Excel/PDF/Slides) · Research · Images · Agents · Connectors (MCP) · Artifacts/Dashboards · Design/Website · Automation · Browser control · Computer use · Plugins

By the end you'll have touched essentially every capability Claude Cowork has.

🧭 LEVEL 0 — FOUND YOUR COMPANY

Block: Memory · 100 XP · Difficulty ●

The story. Every empire starts with a name and a clear reason to exist. Before you build anything, your Guide needs to remember who you are and what you're building — across every future session.

Mission - Sharpen your business idea with the Guide into one crisp sentence (the "north star"). - Name the company. Pick a one-line value proposition and your target customer. - Create a project folder and a memory file capturing: company name, what it does, who it's for, your brand vibe, and your decisions so far.

Victory Condition ✅ You can start a brand-new chat, say "what's my company?", and the Guide recalls it correctly from the memory file — not from the conversation.

Your first step: Tell the Guide your rough idea. Let it interview you with 5 sharp questions. Then you write the memory file.

Stretch challenge. Add a "Decisions Log" section and make a rule: every real choice (pricing, name, tech) → one line appended. This is how pros keep an AI aligned over months.

Teach-back: Why does a memory file beat just having a long conversation?

⚒️ LEVEL 1 — TEACH CLAUDE YOUR CRAFT

Block: Skills · 150 XP · Difficulty ●

The story. You'll do the same tasks over and over — a customer email, a product description, a weekly update. A Skill is you teaching Claude a repeatable workflow once, so it nails it every time.

Mission - Identify one task your business will need constantly. - Build a Skill that captures how you want it done — your voice, your format, your rules. - Save it so it triggers automatically, then use it twice on two different inputs.

Victory Condition ✅ A saved Skill that fires on the right request and produces output in your defined format — without you re-explaining the rules.

Your first step: Pick the task. Tell the Guide. It helps you find the skill-creator tooling — but you write the skill's instructions and examples.

Stretch challenge. Give the Skill one "do NOT" rule and one worked example, then feed it a tricky input to see if it holds the line.

Teach-back: What's the difference between a Skill and a really good one-off prompt?

📄 LEVEL 2 — THE PAPERWORK EMPIRE

Block: Documents · Spreadsheets · PDF · Slides · 200 XP · Difficulty ●

The story. A real business runs on real documents. Cowork doesn't just chat — it makes Word docs, Excel spreadsheets, PDFs, and slide decks. Time to build your company's paperwork.

Mission - Have Cowork create a Word document your business needs (a one-pager, proposal, or service agreement). - Build an Excel spreadsheet with at least one working formula (a pricing sheet, budget, or simple financial model — lean on your finance brain here). - Produce a client-ready PDF (a brochure or invoice). - Build a short slide deck (pptx) — a 5–8 slide pitch for the business.

Victory Condition ✅ Four real files exist — a .docx, an .xlsx with a live formula, a .pdf, and a .pptx — all on-brand for your company.

Your first step: Start with the spreadsheet. Ask the Guide to build a pricing or unit-economics sheet — then you add or fix a formula so a total calculates itself.

Stretch challenge. Make the deck pull its numbers from your spreadsheet, and ask for speaker notes on each slide.

Teach-back: Which of these would save you the most time in a real job, and why?

🔎 LEVEL 3 — KNOW YOUR MARKET

Block: Research · Images / Brand · 200 XP · Difficulty ●●

The story. You can't win a market you don't understand — and you can't stand out without a look. This level is intel plus identity.

Mission - Use Cowork's web research to investigate your market: 3–5 competitors, their pricing, and one gap you could own. Get a sourced summary. - Have Cowork generate images for your brand: a logo and at least one other visual (hero image, icon set, or social graphic). - Save the research to memory and the images to your project.

Victory Condition ✅ A sourced competitive summary in your files, plus a generated logo and a second brand visual you'd actually use.

Your first step: Write the research question tightly and give it to the Guide — then push it for sources, not just claims.

Stretch challenge. Turn the research into a one-paragraph "positioning statement" and log it in your Decisions Log.

Teach-back: How do you tell a trustworthy research summary from a confident-sounding guess?

🤖 LEVEL 4 — HIRE YOUR FIRST AGENT

Block: Agents / Subagents · 200 XP · Difficulty ●●

The story. A founder can't do everything. You delegate. An agent is a specialized AI worker you hand a self-contained job to — it goes off, does the work, and reports back without cluttering your main thread.

Mission - Find a chunky, self-contained job (e.g. "audit 50 product names for trademark risk," or "summarize 10 competitor reviews"). - Launch an agent to do it. - Get back a clean, usable deliverable and save it into your project.

Victory Condition ✅ An agent ran an independent multi-step task and returned a result you actually use in a decision.

Your first step: Write the job brief so tight a worker who knows nothing about your company could run it. That's the real skill.

Stretch challenge. Run two agents in parallel and have the Guide help you combine their outputs.

Teach-back: When is it worth spinning up an agent vs. just asking the Guide directly?

🔌 LEVEL 5 — WIRE UP THE REAL WORLD

Block: Connectors (MCP) · 200 XP · Difficulty ●●

The story. Real businesses touch real tools — email, calendars, files, payments. Connectors (MCP) plug Claude into those tools so it can read and act on live data.

Mission - Decide the one real tool your business most needs Claude connected to. - Use the connector registry to find and connect it (ask the Guide to search — don't assume; verify what's offered). - Do one real thing through it: read your latest emails, pull events, list files, draft (not send) a message.

Victory Condition ✅ Claude pulled or acted on live data from a connected external tool, for a real business purpose.

Your first step: Name the tool and the exact task. The Guide helps you search the registry — but you decide what's worth connecting and why.

⚠️ Safety rule: never let an agent send money, place orders, or send mass emails unattended. Read, draft, and analyze freely — but you press the final button. Tell the Guide you know this rule.

Teach-back: Why is "Claude connected to your real tools" a bigger deal than "Claude that writes good text"?

📊 LEVEL 6 — THE LIVING DASHBOARD

Block: Artifacts / Dashboards · 250 XP · Difficulty ●●●

The story. A founder needs a cockpit — one screen showing the health of the business that refreshes itself. An artifact is a self-contained page Claude builds that persists and pulls fresh data every time you open it.

Mission - Design a one-page dashboard: the 3–5 numbers or lists that actually matter. - Build it as a live artifact. If you cleared Level 5, wire at least one tile to real connector data; otherwise mock it cleanly. - Make it look good, not just functional.

Victory Condition ✅ A reopenable dashboard showing your key metrics, with at least one element that updates from a data source — that you'd genuinely glance at each morning.

Your first step: Sketch the 4 tiles on paper. Defend each to the Guide: "why does this number matter?" Kill any vanity metric.

Stretch challenge. Add a tile that uses Claude to summarize the data in plain English.

Teach-back: What makes a dashboard useful vs. just busy?

🎨 LEVEL 7 — BUILD THE STOREFRONT

Block: Design / Website · 250 XP · Difficulty ●●●

The story. No customer sees your folder. They see your website — and good design separates "student project" from "I'd give them my email." Use Claude's design skills (design critique, UX copy, accessibility) explicitly.

Mission - Build a real landing page: hero with your value prop, what you offer, social proof or how-it-works, and a clear call-to-action. Use your brand images from Level 3. - Make it genuinely well-designed — ask the Guide to use its design-critique skill and act on it. - Get it to where you'd show it to a stranger.

Victory Condition ✅ A polished, responsive landing page that clearly communicates what you do and drives one action — and survives an honest design critique.

Your first step: Write the hero headline and subhead first, before visuals. If the words don't sell it, no design will.

Stretch challenge. Run an accessibility review (contrast, tap targets, readable type) and fix what fails.

Teach-back: Point at one design choice and explain what it does for the visitor.

🌙 LEVEL 8 — THE NIGHT SHIFT

Block: Automation / Scheduled Tasks · 200 XP · Difficulty ●●●

The story. The best businesses make progress while the founder sleeps. A scheduled task runs on its own — a morning briefing, a weekly report, a daily scan.

Mission - Identify something that should happen automatically and repeatedly. - Set up a scheduled task to run it (e.g. every morning at 7am, every Monday). - Have it produce something you'll actually read — ideally pulling from your dashboard, connectors, or memory.

Victory Condition ✅ A scheduled task fires on its own cadence and produces a useful artifact without you lifting a finger.

Your first step: Finish this sentence for the Guide: "Every _, I want to automatically receive _, so I can ____."

Stretch challenge. Chain it: the task uses a Skill from Level 1 and reads your memory file. Now your building blocks work together unattended.

Teach-back: What's the difference between a task you should automate and one you shouldn't?

🕹️ LEVEL 9 — COMMAND THE MACHINE

Block: Browser control · Computer use · Plugins · 250 XP · Difficulty ●●●●

The story. Time for the power tools. Cowork can drive a real web browser, control native desktop apps, and load whole capability bundles called plugins. Use all three for your business.

Mission - Browser (Claude in Chrome): have Cowork do a real task in a live browser — gather info from a site, or fill a form for your business. (Needs the Chrome extension connected.) - Computer use: have Cowork do one small, safe task in a native desktop app. (Depends on your setup — skip if unavailable.) - Plugins: explore available plugins/marketplaces, install one that fits your business, and use something from it.

Victory Condition ✅ You drove a real browser task, tried a desktop task (or noted it's unavailable), and installed & used a plugin.

Your first step: Ask the Guide what's actually available in your setup — browser extension, desktop control, which plugins — then pick the task.

⚠️ Safety rule: verify any link before letting it act; never let it move money, buy, or send on your behalf — you press those buttons.

Teach-back: Which of these three would change how you work the most, and why?

👑 FINAL BOSS — LAUNCH DAY

Block: Everything · 600 XP · Difficulty ●●●●●

The story. This is it. Every system you built must work together as one business.

The boss fight — pass every check: 1. 🧠 Memory — A fresh session, and the Guide can brief a stranger on your whole business from your files. 2. ⚒️ Skill — A core workflow runs through a saved Skill, on-brand, every time. 3. 📄 Documents — You can produce a doc, sheet, PDF, and deck on demand. 4. 🔎 Research + 🖼️ Brand — Your positioning is backed by research; your look is your own. 5. 🤖 Agent — A real piece of work was delegated to an agent and used. 6. 🔌 Connector — Live (or cleanly simulated) data flows from a real tool. 7. 📊 Dashboard + 🎨 Website + 🌙 Automation — Cockpit refreshes, landing page is launch-ready, something runs on a schedule. 8. 🕹️ Power tools — You've used browser control and a plugin (and tried computer use).

Victory Condition ✅ You can give a 5-minute live demo of your business — website, dashboard, an agent doing work, an automation firing, a plugin in play — narrating which Cowork capability powers each piece.

The prize. Ask the Guide to generate your Founder Quest Certificate (your name, your business, your final XP, the date) and a one-page "what I built and learned" summary you could screenshot for a resume or LinkedIn.

🆘 THE HINT LADDER (how to ask the Guide for help)

When you're stuck, ask in this order — the Guide holds back so you keep learning:

  1. "Nudge me." → One sentence pointing you in the right direction.
  2. "Warmer?" → Names the specific tool/concept you need.
  3. "Walk me to the edge." → Steps you through up to the hard part, then hands it back.
  4. "I genuinely need to see it once." → The Guide shows the move once, then gives you a fresh variation to do yourself.

Burning straight to level 4 hints skips the learning. Stretch mode rewards climbing the ladder slowly.

📜 PLAYER RULES OF ENGAGEMENT

  • You type the commands. You write the files. The Guide shows patterns, never does the whole thing.
  • One business, all game. Don't start over — push your idea through every level. Constraints make you creative.
  • Log your decisions. Every real choice → one line in your memory file.
  • Get stuck on purpose. The struggle right before the hint is where the learning lives.
  • Have fun and be bold. Pick the cooler option. Ship the thing. This is a sandbox with real tools.
